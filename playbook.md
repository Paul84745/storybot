# Story playbook

What top story accounts do, and what the writer follows. Edit freely — the
system reads this file every run. Add learnings from your own analytics under
"Our results" (e.g. which story types got the most views or follows).

## Story types that perform
- Moral dilemma ("Am I wrong for...?"): viewers argue in the comments. Make it genuinely debatable.
- Pro revenge / petty revenge: slow-burn setup, clever legal payback, very satisfying ending.
- Malicious compliance: someone demands rules be followed, narrator follows them literally, it backfires on them.
- Entitled family / in-laws / parents: relatable outrage.
- Wedding, inheritance and money betrayal: high stakes, clear villain.
- Secrets and confessions: a hidden truth slowly revealed.
- Workplace power plays: smug boss or coworker exposed.
- Light creepy / unsettling (no gore): something is off and keeps getting worse.

## What a winning premise has
- The whole conflict fits in ONE shocking sentence (e.g. "My sister wore my wedding dress to her own wedding a week before mine").
- Strong emotion: outrage, shock, satisfaction or empathy. Neutral stories die.
- Something specific and unusual — not a generic "my boss was mean".
- Reasonable people could disagree about who was wrong (drives comments).
- A twist the viewer won't see coming.

## Hooks (first 3 seconds)
- Start with the most shocking line of the whole story — never with background or "so this happened".
- Formulas: shocking claim ("I got my own wedding cancelled on purpose"), result first then hold back how,
  confession ("Nobody in my family knows I...") , dilemma ("Am I wrong for refusing to...").
- The on-screen title is the hook in max 9 words, often as a question.

## Pacing
- Short punchy sentences. Dialogue lines. Vivid reactions.
- A new reveal, escalation or twist every 3-4 sentences. Cut backstory before cutting conflict.
- No filler, no moralising, no explaining feelings at length.

## Series structure
- Each part answers one question and raises a bigger one.
- Cut Parts 1 and 2 right BEFORE a major revelation (the moment someone opens the door, reads the message, says the truth).
- Parts 2 and 3 open with a one-line recap of the stakes, then immediately the next beat.
- Part 3 pays off everything, adds one last twist if possible, and ends by asking viewers for their verdict
  ("So... was I wrong?").

## Captions and hashtags
- Mix niche and broad tags (about 6-8): e.g. #storytime #fyp #foryou #drama plus 2-4 topic tags.
- Part 3 caption asks the verdict question to drive comments.

## Avoid
- Real people, brands or places. Violence, gore, sexual content, slurs, illegal revenge.
- Repeating the same type of story two days in a row.
- Slow openings, summaries, morals at the end.
